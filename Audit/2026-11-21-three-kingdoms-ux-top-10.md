# SimaYi’s top 10 Three Kingdoms UX findings

Review `AUD-008`, based on published playtest commit `6e62a91`. SimaYi ranked the items from a source review. “Source-confirmed” means the behavior was observed in code. Player impact still needs playtest evidence. KongMing corrected the long-battle comparison to match the live 20–100 wave choices.


| Rank | ID | Evidence | Finding and player impact | Validation or improvement |
|---:|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | GF-020 | Playtest hypothesis. Off-road tiles resemble ordinary terrain and the idle map has no build cue. | **First placement is easy to miss.** A new player may tap the road or start without defenses. | Observe first tap, wrong taps, and time to first placement. Compare a brief “Tap grass to build” cue. |
| 2 | GF-021 | Source-confirmed. Range drawing requires an existing tower. | **New-tower coverage is hidden before purchase.** Players cannot judge which bend a new Archer or Drum will cover. | Preview both footprints with distinct line styles. Verify the two-tap purchase still works. |
| 3 | GF-022 | Source-confirmed. Escaping attackers disappear and lives decrease without an on-map gate-hit cue. | **Escapes give little gate feedback.** Players have little evidence about which threat slipped through. | Show a brief gate-hit cue identifying the attacker and verify players can explain the lost life. |
| 4 | GF-023 | Playtest hypothesis. The tray sits below the full map and round controls. | **Purchase cards can be hard to find on short phones.** A player may miss the options or lose sight of the selected tile when scrolling. | At 320 × 640 and 390 × 740, observe whether players find both cards and retain selection context. |
| 5 | GF-024 | Source-confirmed. At 3×, the 0.25-second impact cue lasts about 83 ms and the 0.7-second payout cue about 233 ms. | **Triple speed compresses combat feedback.** Hits and earned silver can flash past. | Compare recognition at 1× and 3×. Give key visual cues a minimum display time independent of simulation speed. |
| 6 | GF-025 | Playtest hypothesis. Slowed enemies show a ring but the Drum has no matching activation cue. | **The Drum’s contribution is hard to trace.** Players may miss why an Archer–Drum pairing works. | Compare a subtle pulse from the Drum and ask players afterward to explain the pairing. |
| 7 | GF-026 | Source-confirmed. Enemy movement and silhouette distinguish runners, while veteran rank changes head tint. | **Veterans rely on a color cue.** Tougher attackers are harder to identify at phone scale. | Give veterans a distinct helmet or shoulder silhouette and test all four types in grayscale. |
| 8 | GF-027 | Source-confirmed. Combat advances automatically and the HUD number is the main wave-boundary cue. | **Wave transitions have little visible emphasis.** Players may miss when a new wave and retry snapshot begin. | Test a brief wave-start cue while combat continues uninterrupted. |
| 9 | GF-028 | Playtest hypothesis. The same attacker sequence repeats and towers and silver carry forward. The live choices are 20, 40, 60, 80, and 100 waves. | **Long battles may add waiting rather than decisions.** A strong opening may leave too little reason to adapt. | Compare 20- and 40-wave runs. Record the last meaningful purchase or placement change and when players disengage. |
| 10 | GF-029 | Source-confirmed. The battlefield is named in its accessibility label, while the visible entry and gate have no text labels. | **The Shu theme could orient players on the map.** Players lack visible landmarks for what they are defending and where attackers enter. | Add an Entry banner and a compact Shu Outpost gate label. Check that both can be identified before starting. |
