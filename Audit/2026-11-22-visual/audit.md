## Visual UX audit · live `ec46433`

The strongest explanation for the “1990 and amateurish” reaction is the jump from illustrated menus to a battlefield that looks like a level editor. The concept puts Late Han architecture, terrain, and recognizable troops where the player spends most of their time. The live build puts most of that character in two cropped illustrations.

I captured the private live build in touch-enabled Chromium at **390 × 740** and **320 × 480**, covering the menu, briefing, placement, and combat with an Archer and Drum. The live JavaScript matched the active `ec46433` release. [Concept screens](/srv/orrery/workspaces/3guotd/references/late-han-visual-proposal/screens.png) · [Art sheet](/srv/orrery/workspaces/3guotd/references/late-han-visual-proposal/art-sheet.png)

| Rank | Finding and evidence | Root cause and proposed change |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **The battlefield has lost the setting.** The concept shows a road through a village, with fields, walls, and a gate that feels worth defending. Live combat shows an olive checkerboard, a uniform road, and a small gate glyph. [Live combat](review-390-combat.png) | The canvas draws terrain cell by cell and builds the road and gate from simple shapes. Start with one illustrated **Village Watch** battlefield aligned to the existing route and hit grid. Give the gate and entry distinct silhouettes, and put scenery outside the lane so it stays readable. |
| **2** | **Combatants and towers read as symbols.** The art sheet gives the Archer a raised platform, the Drum a timber stand, and troops distinct equipment. In the live capture, attackers read mostly as dark blobs with health bars, and the card portraits repeat the simplified tower shapes. [Combat](review-390-combat.png) · [Purchase cards](review-320-placement.png) | Canvas primitives carry nearly all the in-battle art. Make purpose-built sprites from the approved silhouettes and share them between the board and cards. Review them **at actual phone scale** in a frame with several moving enemies, health bars, and active effects. |
| **3** | **The map shrinks just when the player needs precision.** At 320 × 480, selecting an empty tile reduced the board from **219 to 180 px wide** and the cell from about **27 to 23 px**. The route stays visible and the cards fit, but the selected tile and landmarks move as the map resizes. [Setup](review-320-setup.png) · [Placement](review-320-placement.png) | The short-screen layout takes space for the tray out of the board. Prototype a shallower contextual tray and a stable map size across selection states. Recheck edge taps, both purchase cards, refund, and the zero-silver message together at 320 × 480. |
| **4** | **Build markers compete with the battlefield.** The concept shows stakes as part of the land. Live places four identical corner strokes on every eligible empty cell over a visible checkerboard, creating a dense field of brackets even in combat. [Live combat](review-390-combat.png) | The markers are a uniform CSS overlay. Keep the visible placement cue, soften the permanent grid, and use small terrain-integrated stakes with stronger emphasis on selection. Verify that an uncoached player still finds a legal tile and can distinguish it from the road. |
| **5** | **The main menu looks like a settings form.** The concept separates large play actions from smaller session controls. Live gives Campaign, Long battle, disabled save actions, and Sound the same two-column treatment inside a small panel, leaving much of the taller screen blank. [Live menu](review-390-menu.png) | The illustration is a banner above a uniform button grid. Use the gate art as part of the composition, give **Continue** or **Campaign** clear priority, and group save, restore, restart, and sound as secondary controls with readable labels. |
| **6** | **The briefing buries the mission under setup controls.** The concept leads with the mission name, the villagers, and one clear objective. Live leads with the product title and two native selectors, crops the illustration into a strip, and presents the objective as one line of body copy. [Live briefing](review-390-briefing.png) | Mission selection and briefing have equal visual weight. Keep the selectors compact, lead with **Village Watch** and the hold objective, and use a phone-specific crop that preserves the gate and people. Keep **Enter battle** visible at 320 × 480 with **Ready offline** shown. |

I’d start with **one complete Village Watch combat screen** at both phone sizes before extending the art to the other maps. That would test the biggest visual gap while preserving the verified placement and purchase flows. This was a visual and interaction audit. First-time comprehension remains untested.
