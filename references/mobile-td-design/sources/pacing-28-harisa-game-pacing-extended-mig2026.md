## Abstract

Game pacing shapes player experience, yet the term often lacks a shared basis for analysis and design. This study defines pacing as the temporal organization of gameplay experience and extends earlier pacing-based generation work into a framework connecting gameplay measures, pacing aspects, and profiles. The framework provides a common vocabulary, analysis at multiple timescales, and a reusable procedure that distinguishes intended, observed, and player-experienced pacing. We demonstrate its use through controlled agent gameplay in _Sumobot_ and procedurally generated levels in _Cute Souls_, with _Assassin’s Creed Shadows_ and _Dota 2_ illustrating applications to other gameplay contexts. The cases show how profiles reveal temporal patterns in agent behavior and generated level structure. Comparisons depend on compatible aspect definitions, segmentation, and normalization, while absolute values remain specific to these choices. The framework supports clearer communication of design intent and structured pacing analysis, and enables high-level pacing comparisons while facilitating flexible, genre-specific implementations. We also discuss potential applications to adaptive design and implications for player well-being.
